# Complaints, recovery and continuous improvement

This proposed process uses complaints, reports, research and service evidence to improve access, satisfactory outcomes and the credibility of a brand promise. It supplies a process to adapt, not an already operating complaint service, legal deadline or enforcement mechanism. Read the [framework](POURS_Access_Experience_and_Brand_Promise.md) and [EAA mapping](POURS_and_EAA_Annex_I_Mapping.md).

## What a complaint can and cannot tell us

A complaint may reveal a blocked task, unmet expectation, unsafe condition, loss of dignity or broken promise. It can be made by an affected person, a representative or someone reporting a risk to others. It is evidence to investigate, not automatic proof of either a technical breach or a measured feeling.

**No complaints is not evidence of satisfaction.** People may not find the route, be unable to use it, fear consequences, lack time, abandon the service or expect nothing to change. Complaint volume depends on access to reporting as well as the underlying experience. An increase after introducing an accessible channel may mean better visibility rather than worsening access.

**Satisfaction does not override non-conformity.** A person may be pleased with an alternative while the required route still fails. Conversely, dissatisfaction can concern an experience outside a particular accessibility requirement. Keep both matters visible and route them to appropriate owners.

## EAA responsibilities and the proposed organisational response

The [Directive’s official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj) distinguishes service-provider duties in Article 13, Member State service-compliance procedures in Article 23, and enforcement in Article 29. Article 23 includes follow-up of complaints or reports about service non-compliance and verification of corrective action. It does not establish a general satisfaction standard.

Check the relevant national implementation, competent authority, product/service scope and sector obligations. The process below is Double FW governance guidance. Assign real owners and agreed response times locally, with legal review where needed. Do not delay urgent protective or legally required action while waiting for a satisfaction study or committee meeting. A commercial preference cannot waive an applicable obligation.

## Proposed process

| Step | Proposed action | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Receive accessibly | Provide discoverable, usable reporting routes and choice of communication method; accept a report without demanding a diagnosis | Date, route, communication preference, task and a minimised account |
| 2. Acknowledge and protect | Confirm receipt, explain next steps in an accessible form and identify urgent harm or loss | Actual owner and target response time; immediate protective action where warranted |
| 3. Separate the findings | Record the functional barrier, experience outcome, reported emotion and brand-promise concern separately | What was reported, what was observed, what is inferred, and what is unknown |
| 4. Investigate | Reproduce safely in relevant conditions; inspect linked routes, third-party dependencies and shared components | Version, access method, task state, applicable requirement and evidence gaps |
| 5. Restore and correct | Offer a workable interim route where appropriate and address the underlying cause | Chosen/forced support, remaining harm, corrective work, owner and due date |
| 6. Retest | Verify the affected task and related routes after the change; invite voluntary feedback through a usable method | Correct end state, regression checks, unresolved issues and limits of testing |
| 7. Close or keep open explicitly | Explain what changed, what remains and applicable escalation options | Reasoned decision, participant notification preference and reopen conditions |
| 8. Learn across the service | Feed patterns into design, content, procurement, policy and programme work | Shared cause, affected components/journeys, decision and follow-up review |

A temporary alternative is not proof that the original route is fixed. Do not require the complainant to retest to receive a remedy or to prevent closure of a legal issue. If the person does not respond, record that limitation rather than infer satisfaction. Repair verification and the person’s view of recovery are separate findings.

## Triage by impact and evidence

Consider blocked essential tasks, financial or safety consequences, privacy, irreversibility, reach, recurrence and availability of an effective alternative. Low complaint volume does not mean low impact. Do not assign low severity to Satisfactory simply because it concerns experience: an exhausting recovery route can cause serious exclusion or loss.

Where evidence is incomplete, investigate and apply proportionate protection; “unknown” is not “passed”. A proposed reviewer or on-call role in `policies.yaml` is a label for local adoption, not proof that a team exists or has been notified. Triggers and action strings are data, never executable instructions or automatic decisions.

## Minimum case record

Use pseudonymous identifiers and separate contact details from the working evidence where practical. Record only what is needed:

- Case ID, date, channel, communication preference and relevant consent or processing arrangements.
- Product/service, version, task and intended end state; relevant access method/settings without unnecessary diagnosis.
- Reporter’s account, observed evidence and researcher/handler inference in separate fields.
- Applicable legal/technical references pending specialist review; linked POURS and SX categories.
- Functional finding, satisfactory-outcome finding and emotional/brand finding, each with evidence or “not assessed”.
- Interim route and whether assistance was chosen or forced; any unresolved consequences.
- Owner, priority rationale, actions, dates, retest evidence and decision communicated.
- External referral or enforcement information where appropriate, retention/access restrictions and reopen reason.

Do not put raw case records, personal disclosures, tokens or recordings in this public repository, issue tracker or Hugging Face dataset. Publish only blank templates, fictional examples or reviewed learning with an appropriate rights/privacy basis. Reporters should not have to consent to model training to obtain help.

## Review signals without gaming the measure

Track accessibility of the reporting routes, acknowledgement and resolution times with clear definitions, repeated contacts, reopened cases, forced assistance, task restoration and recurring causes. Where useful, report rates with their denominator and periods, not raw counts alone. Distinguish no cases from missing data.

Invite optional feedback on recovery. Report question wording, scale, response count and non-response. Do not reward handlers for suppressing complaints, closing cases early or securing favourable ratings. A single severe barrier can warrant action even when the overall dashboard looks positive.

Research should also reach people who abandoned, could not complain or never entered the service. Combine their accounts with technical testing and observed outcomes. No one evidence source represents all customers.

## Learning and accountability

Use the [Accessibility Programme Runbooks](https://github.com/Double-FW/Accessibility_Programme_Runbooks) to connect improvement work to programme and service responsibilities. The [EAA monitoring guide](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach/blob/main/guides/06-monitoring-and-corrective-action.md) supports evidence-led correction. [Synchronistic Design](https://github.com/Double-FW/Synchronistic-Design-Formal-Operating-Model) connects intent, action and feedback; the [brand research guide](Brand_Values_and_Inclusive_Experience_Research.md) tests whether the promise survives recovery.

Before adopting this process, confirm owners, accessible channels, response and escalation rules, privacy arrangements and resources. Then pilot it with affected people and test both the reporting route and an actual corrective-work handover. Documentation checks alone do not establish operational readiness.
