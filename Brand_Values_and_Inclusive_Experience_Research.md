# Brand values and inclusive experience research

A brand promise is a proposition to test in the lived experience of a service. This proposed research approach connects an organisation’s own values to functional access, satisfactory outcomes and the five emotional lenses in the [POURS framework](POURS_Access_Experience_and_Brand_Promise.md). It does not assume that values cause feelings, loyalty or commercial benefit.

## Start with the organisation’s actual promise

Record the value in its approved wording, its source, version and scope. Ask who is authorised to explain it and which decisions it is meant to influence. Distinguish a published promise from a researcher’s proposed interpretation. This repository does not supply approved values for another organisation.

Translate each value into observable commitments and possible contradictions. Include recruitment, marketing, purchase, onboarding, everyday use, support, complaints, renewal and exit where relevant. A promise of control has little credibility if cancellation removes saved work or requires an inaccessible telephone call.

## Research opportunities

| Opportunity | Question | Evidence and caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promise discovery | What do people expect the stated value to mean in this encounter? | Ask before showing intended emotional labels; allow unfamiliarity or disagreement |
| Inclusive concept research | Which proposed behaviours would meet different needs? | Co-design with affected people, including conflicting preferences; do not claim unanimity |
| Journey research | Where does the promise hold or break across routes and handovers? | Observe consequential tasks, waiting, abandonment, support and exit |
| Recovery research | Does the response restore access and respect after a failure? | Separate task restoration, participant experience and unresolved consequences |
| Brand differentiation | What does the organisation do that participants recognise as distinctive? | Compare specific behaviours and alternatives without suggesting a preferred brand answer |
| Research over time | Does the experience remain dependable as circumstances and systems change? | Repeated studies with context and attrition reported; association is not causation |
| Governance research | Which patterns expose a gap between promise, policy and delivery? | Combine consented research with minimised complaint themes and technical evidence |

## Promise-to-evidence worksheet

Complete this for each material promise. The worked example is fictional and supplies no measured results.

| Field | Illustrative entry |
| --- | --- |
| Brand value and provenance | “People stay in control”; fictional value for this example |
| Intended promise | People can decide their pace and leave without avoidable loss |
| Observable behaviour | Save progress, pause, resume and cancel through a usable route |
| Functional question | Do controls, feedback and saved state work with the selected access method? |
| Experience question | Can the person complete, pause or exit with acceptable effort and recover their place? |
| Emotional inquiry | How did this encounter affect their sense of agency, confidence or being valued, if at all? |
| Disconfirming evidence | Forced support, unannounced loss of work, pressured choices or indifference to the promise |
| Research conditions | Task, version, access method, context, chosen support and sample limitations recorded |
| Decision and retest | Owner to be confirmed; change the service behaviour and test the affected route again |

Do not replace the functional evidence with a favourable response to the emotional inquiry. Equally, a passing technical test cannot establish what the person felt.

## An accessible study sequence

1. **Set the question.** Select one real promise, one consequential journey and a decision the evidence can influence. Agree which failures require action irrespective of ratings.
2. **Plan participation.** Involve disabled people and people with relevant access needs as contributors. Offer accessible invitations, consent materials, scheduling, communication, participation modes, breaks, support and fair compensation. Include people who could not enter or complete the service.
3. **Agree data boundaries.** Collect the minimum information needed; access method and task context may be enough. Explain purpose, recording, retention, access, withdrawal limits and any proposed reuse. Obtain appropriate consent for participation and quotations; confirm the separate lawful basis for processing with the responsible organisation.
4. **Observe the journey.** Let people use their normal methods where feasible. Record consequential errors, help, waiting and end states. Do not deliberately expose participants to hazards to test whether they feel safe.
5. **Ask openly.** First ask what happened and what mattered. Then explore expectations and the brand promise. Offer the five lenses as optional prompts after the open account; accept “none”, other language and disagreement.
6. **Analyse the three lenses separately.** Keep observed access, satisfactory outcomes and reported feelings distinct. Preserve negative cases, missing evidence, conflicting preferences and researcher interpretations.
7. **Decide and return.** Document the decision, owner, change and retest. Tell participants what changed in an accessible form if they opted into follow-up. Research participation does not oblige them to endorse the organisation.

## Questions that avoid leading the answer

| Lens | Optional neutral prompt |
| --- | --- |
| Feeling Seen | What information or participation, if any, was available to you or missing? |
| Feeling Capable | Where could you act as you intended, and where did you need help or another route? |
| Feeling Confident | What did you understand about your position, the result and the next step? |
| Feeling Safe | What made this experience dependable or uncertain for you? |
| Feeling Valued | What conveyed consideration or disregard for your needs and choices, if anything? |

Do not ask “Did this accessible design make you feel valued?” Treat answers as specific accounts, not diagnoses or automatic scores for a whole brand. Feeling safe is not proof of physical, information or financial safety. Do not infer emotion from clicks, facial expressions, voice, assistive technology or disability labels.

## Reporting, ownership and evidence limits

A report should identify the promise and journey, study conditions, recruitment gaps, sample, access methods, outcomes, participant accounts, contradictions, limitations and decisions. Report denominators and non-completion. Avoid small demographic breakdowns or quotations that could identify someone. Validate summaries with contributors where practical without requiring approval of the brand interpretation.

Proposed responsibilities are a brand owner for the promise, research lead for study quality, accessibility specialist for applicable technical evidence, service owner for repair, and privacy/legal specialists for their own domains. Confirm actual people, authority and resources before adoption. A YAML role name does not establish an operating team.

The [Synchronistic Design operating model](https://github.com/Double-FW/Synchronistic-Design-Formal-Operating-Model) supplies a useful connection between intent, context, action and feedback. [Cognitive UX](https://github.com/Double-FW/Cognitive-UX-Design) supplies comprehension and control lenses. [Data Ethics and Disability](https://github.com/Double-FW/Data_Ethics_and_Disability) informs participation and data care. [Accessibility Programme Runbooks](https://github.com/Double-FW/Accessibility_Programme_Runbooks) connects recommendations to programme work. These are public reference links; they are not imported research findings.

## Publication and model-training boundary

Publish methods, blank templates, fictional examples and carefully reviewed aggregate learning where rights and privacy permit. Do not add raw interviews, customer records, diagnostic details, recordings or identifiable complaints to this public repository or its Hugging Face export. A source link does not authorise copying another repository’s content or applying this repository’s MIT licence to it.

The framework is currently a proposal. There is no validated five-feeling scale, causal brand-impact model, loyalty prediction or completed participant study supplied here. Testing those claims would require an appropriate study design and evidence beyond documentation checks.
