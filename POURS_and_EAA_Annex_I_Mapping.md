# POURS and EAA Annex I: functional and emotional mapping

This is a **selective research and traceability aid**, not a complete legal checklist, legal opinion, conformity assessment or additional statutory standard. POURS is Double FW’s proposed experience framework. Satisfactory and the five feelings do not add a fifth principle to WCAG or a general satisfaction requirement to the EAA.

## Source, scope and applicability

The primary source is [Directive (EU) 2019/882, official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj), particularly Annex I and Articles 2, 4, 13, 14, 23 and 29. This document’s source review is dated 15 November 2026. The Directive’s original text is the reference for the mapping; relevant national implementing law, subsequent changes, applicable standards and sector rules require a jurisdiction-specific check before reliance.

Before assessing anything, identify the product or service, country/market, economic-operator role, dates, applicable transitional provisions and any supported exemption or Article 14 assessment. Do not assume every website, brand interaction or employer service is in EAA scope. Record applicability as **unconfirmed** until a competent owner has resolved it. A local framework cannot grant an exemption.

Annex I is not interchangeable with WCAG or EN 301 549. Those standards may support technical assessment in an appropriate context; do not assume a WCAG result establishes complete EAA conformity or a presumption without checking the relevant legal basis and scope.

## Annex I route map

| Section | Subject and use in this mapping |
| --- | --- |
| I | Requirements for covered products: information, interface/functionality and available support. Relevant also to products used in service provision through III(a) |
| II | Additional packaging and instructions requirements for covered products other than the specified self-service terminals |
| III | General requirements for covered services, subject to Article 4’s scope and exceptions: products used, information, websites/apps and available support |
| IV | Additional requirements for specific services; select the relevant sector and exact provision |
| V | Answering 112 emergency communications; requires its own specialist assessment, not a general customer-service checklist |
| VI | Accessibility requirements for features/functions in the Article 24(2) relationship with other Union acts; not an extra generic brand test |
| VII | Functional performance criteria, used subject to its stated conditions; not an unrestricted substitute for Sections I–VI |

## Dual-lens mapping

Emotional inquiries below are research hypotheses. They are not legal tests or predictable psychological consequences. Several POURS domains may apply to the same provision; the selected links are illustrative rather than exhaustive.

| POURS lens | Selected Annex I anchor | Functional investigation | Experience and emotional inquiry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Perceivable / Feeling Seen | I.1; I.2(a),(c)–(g); III(b)(i)–(vi) | Can people access meaningful information and alternatives through the relevant methods? | Was important meaning missing, delayed or available only through someone else? Did participation feel possible? |
| Operable / Feeling Capable | I.2(h),(i),(l),(m); III(c); relevant IV provisions | Can the person operate controls, identify themselves, choose a route and complete the task with sufficient time? | Could they act as intended, recover and make meaningful choices without forced assistance? |
| Understandable / Feeling Confident | I.1(a)(ii), I.1(b)(ii); I.2(m); III(b)(ii),(vii), III(c); IV(e)(ii) for consumer banking | Are instructions, states, consequences and errors understandable and consistent? | Did the person know what had happened and what to do next? What remained uncertain? |
| Robust / Feeling Safe | I.1(b)(ix); I.2(n); III(b)(vii), III(c); relevant interoperability provisions in IV | Does the relevant function work across the stated assistive technology, platform and version combinations? | Could they rely on the experience through changes and handovers? Separately check actual safety, security and privacy |
| Satisfactory / Feeling Valued | No general satisfaction clause. Investigate outcomes alongside applicable I–IV requirements and support in I.3 or III(d), where relevant | Did access produce the intended result, with acceptable effort, choice and recovery? | Did the encounter meet the person’s needs without diminishing dignity or identity? Record dissatisfaction even when technical checks pass |

“Robust” does not own every safety obligation: photosensitive-seizure safeguards and privacy require direct attention, including I.2(j),(k) and relevant VII criteria. A feeling of trust cannot demonstrate those protections. Nor can a complaint establish a particular clause failure without investigation.

## Sector examples: select rather than generalise

| Annex I provision | Illustrative task and additional experience inquiry |
| --- | --- |
| IV(a), electronic communications | Check the relevant real-time text, video/total-conversation and emergency-transmission requirements; investigate whether communication can proceed without forced voice use |
| IV(b), access to audiovisual media services | Check guides and transmission/control of access services; investigate discoverability, synchronisation and the person’s ability to choose and consume content |
| IV(c),(d), relevant transport services | Select the correct transport category and its provisions; investigate usable disruption information or terminals without assuming every category has identical obligations |
| IV(e), consumer banking | Check identification, security/payment and information requirements; investigate comprehension, correct outcome and recovery |
| IV(f), e-books | Check structure, navigation, presentation choice, assistive technology, metadata and rights-management barriers; investigate sustained content access and retained place |
| IV(g), e-commerce | Check applicable accessibility information and identification/security/payment functions; investigate correct purchase, consent and confirmation |

These examples establish no finding about an actual service. Specialised domains, including emergency communications, need specialist review before testing or making claims.

## Section VII: functional performance criteria

Section VII addresses relevant functions not addressed by Sections I–VI. Its use as an alternative to specific technical requirements is conditional, including the required equivalent or increased accessibility. It is not permission to replace an inconvenient requirement with a satisfaction interview.

The criteria below are **task-condition lenses**, not eleven population categories or diagnostic labels. People may use several modes; needs can fluctuate and intersect. Protect privacy throughout, not only in the final row.

| VII criterion | Functional research focus | Possible emotional inquiry; never a statutory proxy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) Without vision | Complete the relevant task without visual information | Was meaning available and action dependable? |
| (b) Limited vision | Use the relevant magnification, contrast and presentation conditions | Was the experience comfortable to follow without losing place? |
| (c) Without colour perception | Interpret information and state without relying on colour | Was the intended meaning clear rather than uncertain? |
| (d) Without hearing | Receive information and complete communication without audio dependence | Was the person included in the whole exchange? |
| (e) Limited hearing | Use relevant enhanced audio features and alternatives | Could they participate on their chosen terms? |
| (f) Without vocal capability | Operate and communicate without required speech | Was agency preserved without compelled disclosure? |
| (g) Limited manipulation or strength | Operate through the relevant alternative control method | Was effort reasonable and control effective? |
| (h) Limited reach | Use the relevant functions without extensive reach | Was the route usable without avoidable dependence? |
| (i) Photosensitive-seizure risk | Review and test applicable safeguards safely; do not expose participants to hazards | Was control and reassurance present, separately from verified risk reduction? |
| (j) Limited cognition | Examine comprehension, predictability, recognition, continuity and flexible pace | Did the person understand the state and feel able to continue? |
| (k) Privacy | Examine privacy when accessibility features are used | Did the person retain dignity, choice and control over disclosure? |

## Evidence and decision record

For each assessed requirement, record the legal source/version, exact clause, applicability rationale, product/service and version, task and conditions, method, evidence reference, result, gaps, responsible reviewer and corrective work. Keep legal/conformity conclusions separate from observed SX outcomes and voluntary emotional accounts.

Use a jurisdiction-approved assessment method for any conformity status. This mapping itself leaves applicability and conformity **not assessed**. A source citation, YAML trigger, favourable satisfaction rating or absence of complaints cannot change that status.

## Complaints and governance

Article 23 concerns Member State procedures to check service compliance, follow up complaints or reports of non-compliance, and verify corrective action. Article 13 sets service-provider obligations, including continued conformity and action when non-conformity is identified. Article 29 concerns effective enforcement routes under national law. These roles must not be collapsed into an invented universal company complaint deadline.

Complaints make dissatisfaction and possible exclusion visible. They justify investigation; they do not turn all dissatisfaction into an EAA breach or make satisfaction an implied legal conformity test. People may report a risk on behalf of others, and many dissatisfied people cannot or choose not to complain. Read the [complaints guide](Complaints_Recovery_and_Continuous_Improvement.md).

For the wider Double FW approach, consult the public [EAA repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/European-Accessibility-Act-EAA-Compliance-Approach), particularly its customer-outcomes and monitoring guides. Its organisational outcome identifiers and this POURS mapping remain distinct from statutory clauses.
